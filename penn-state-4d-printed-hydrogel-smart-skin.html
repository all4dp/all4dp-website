Penn State prints a hydrogel “smart skin” that hides images and changes shape

A halftone printing pattern lets one hydrogel sheet control its look, its texture and its 3D form together.

11 February 2026 · 2 min read · all4dp editorial

Illustration: all4dp

Engineers at Penn State, working with Georgia Tech, have 4D printed a hydrogel film that can change its shape, surface texture and appearance in response to its surroundings. The team calls it a smart synthetic skin.

How it works

The method borrows from newspaper printing. Image or texture data is converted into a halftone pattern of binary regions across the hydrogel. Each region is set to respond differently, so the pattern decides how the whole sheet behaves when it is heated, exposed to a solvent or stretched.

What they showed

  • A single film stored a hidden image that appeared after immersion in ice water or on heating.
  • Information could also be revealed by mechanical deformation.
  • Flat sheets curved into complex, textured 3D shapes without multiple layers or multiple materials.

Why it matters

Shape change in printed hydrogels usually needs several materials or layers. Here optical, mechanical and shape functions are designed together in one sheet of one material. The authors point to adaptive camouflage, information encryption, soft robotics, wearables and biomedical systems as possible uses.

The project was led by Hongtao Sun, assistant professor of industrial and manufacturing engineering at Penn State, with doctoral candidate Haoqing Yang as first author and H. Jerry Qi of Georgia Tech among the collaborators.

Sources

  • Nature Communications (peer-reviewed paper), Penn State and Georgia Tech
