4D-printed stents from Waseda open at body temperature, no heater needed

A plasticiser tunes the shape memory polymer so the stent recovers at about 37 °C.

6 February 2026 · 2 min read · all4dp editorial

Illustration: all4dp

A group led by Waseda University in Japan has 4D printed coronary artery stents that expand by themselves at body temperature. Earlier shape memory stents generally needed external heating to deploy, which is hard to do safely inside a blood vessel.

The material

The stents are made from a polycaprolactone-based shape memory polymer composite and printed by projection micro-stereolithography, a high-resolution process that cures resin with patterned ultraviolet light. The key step is adding diethyl phthalate as a plasticiser. This lowers the polymer's thermal transition to about 37 °C, so normal body heat is enough to trigger recovery.

What was tested

  • Computer simulations and a stress-relaxation model indicated a good balance between flexibility and radial strength.
  • Laboratory tests with human umbilical cells showed good compatibility with cells.
  • Implantation in mice supported the potential for clinical use.

The report does not give numerical values for these results.

Why it matters

A stent that can be compressed, delivered and then left to open in place could simplify minimally invasive procedures, and printing allows the geometry to be matched to a patient's vessel. The same approach may suit other implants.

This is early-stage work. No human trials have been announced. Collaborators include the University of Tokyo, Southeast University, South China University of Technology and Anhui Medical University.
