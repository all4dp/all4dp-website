IISc team 4D prints a self-folding breast duct model to study drug resistance

A flat printed sheet rolls into a duct-like tube in liquid, giving cancer cells a more realistic place to grow.

13 August 2026 · 2 min read · all4dp editorial

Illustration: all4dp

Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science have used 4D printing to build a small tube that resembles a breast duct, the structure where some breast cancers begin. The aim is a laboratory model that behaves more like real tissue than cells grown on a flat dish.

How it works

The material is printed with visible light as a flat sheet. When the sheet is placed in liquid it folds by itself into a tube. Triple-negative breast cancer cells were then grown inside the tubes under two conditions: still culture, and gentle rocking that imitates fluid flow.

What they found

  • Cells stayed highly viable in both conditions.
  • Under rocking, cells showed higher metabolic activity and changed their shape and organisation.
  • Cells under rocking were also more likely to survive treatment with the chemotherapy drug doxorubicin.

The release does not give numerical values for these measurements.

Why it matters

Tissue shape, stiffness and fluid movement all influence how tumours respond to drugs, and flat cultures capture none of them. A self-folding printed tube adds curvature and flow without complicated assembly. The researchers are clear that this is a research tool: it is not a treatment and does not replace patient testing, but it could help screen therapies in more realistic tumour models.

Funding: Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF), Government of India.
