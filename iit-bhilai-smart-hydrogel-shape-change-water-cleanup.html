IIT Bhilai hydrogel changes shape on heating and pulls pollutants out of water

One printable gel does two jobs: it works as a soft actuator and it removes dyes and lead from wastewater.

15 September 2026 · 2 min read · all4dp editorial

Illustration: all4dp

Researchers at the Indian Institute of Technology Bhilai have reported a hydrogel that combines two functions usually handled by separate materials. It holds and recovers a programmed shape when heated, and it also captures pollutants from water.

What they made

The gel is built from a four-arm star polymer combined with acrylic acid, acrylamide and polyethylene glycol diacrylate (PEGDA). It sets quickly under ultraviolet-A light, which makes it suitable for forming into structures, including by 4D printing. The team's demonstration piece was a flower-like structure that opens when heated.

The numbers

  • Shape fixity of about 94% and shape recovery of about 85%.
  • Return to the programmed shape in roughly 25 seconds.
  • Performance held over repeated shape-change cycles.
  • In one test, 1 gram of dried hydrogel repeatedly lifted a 250-gram metal load.

The same material removed the organic dyes methylene blue, rhodamine B and eosin B, as well as toxic lead ions, from water.

Why it matters

Most shape-changing hydrogels are designed for motion alone. A gel that can also clean water points to soft devices that move to where they are needed and then do useful chemical work. The authors suggest soft robotics, smart actuators and environmental remediation as target uses, and describe the system as a base for future 4D-printable materials.

The work is at laboratory scale. Durability in real wastewater and performance in larger printed parts have not been reported.

Sources

  • Small Methods (peer-reviewed paper), researchers Sudipta Paul, Priyank Sinha, Amul Jain and Dr. Sanjib Banerjee
