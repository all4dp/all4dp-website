Concordia shapes carbon fibre wind turbine blades without moulds

Flat laminates curve into blade shapes as they cool, giving blades about 80% lighter than aluminium ones.

15 July 2026 · 2 min read · all4dp editorial

Illustration: all4dp

A team at Concordia University in Montreal has used 4D printing of composites to make curved blades for vertical-axis wind turbines from flat carbon fibre panels. No curved mould is needed.

The idea

Curved turbine blades are normally made with specialised forming processes and complex moulds, which add cost, time and weight. The Concordia group works backwards instead. They start from the blade shape they want and calculate the arrangement and orientation of carbon fibre layers that will produce it. The flat laminate is cured, and as it cools the engineered differences between layers pull it into the target curve.

Results

  • The blades closely matched the shape of commercial aluminium blades.
  • They weighed about 80% less than the aluminium versions.
  • In laboratory tests, turbines fitted with the composite blades rotated faster.

Why it matters

This is 4D printing at structural scale, with a stiff engineering material and a trigger that is already part of the process: the cool-down after curing. If the method transfers to production, it could cut tooling costs for curved composite parts well beyond wind energy.

The tests were done in the laboratory. Field performance and fatigue life were not covered in the report.
